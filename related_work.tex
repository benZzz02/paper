\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Weakly Aligned Video-Language Pretraining}

Large-scale vision-language models show that contrastive learning from natural language supervision can yield transferable visual representations~\cite{clip2021}. Video-language pretraining extends this idea to narrated or captioned videos, where supervision is often weakly aligned rather than precisely trimmed. HowTo100M and MIL-NCE demonstrated that noisy narrations can supervise video representation learning at scale~\cite{miech2019howto100m,milnce2020}, while VideoCLIP, Frozen-in-Time, HD-VILA, Panda-70M, and Vid2Seq further improved video-text pretraining with larger corpora, stronger encoders, or dense captioning objectives~\cite{videoclip2021,frozenintime2021,pan2022hdvila,wang2024panda70m,vid2seq2023}. In instructional video, several works explicitly exploit narration structure to learn procedure-aware representations~\cite{alayrac2015unsupervised,xu2023procedure_aware_repr,zhou2023procedure_aware_pretraining}. These works establish that weak video-text pairs are useful at scale, but they typically do not ask how a semantically valid interval should be consumed when its decisive visual evidence is temporally sparse or locally shifted. SurgAlign focuses on that intra-pair supervision problem.

\subsection{Weak Temporal Supervision and Evidence Selection}

Our framing is related to multiple-instance learning, where a positive bag may contain only a subset of truly positive instances~\cite{dietterich1997mil,ilse2018attentionmil}. In weakly supervised temporal alignment, grounding, and action localization, researchers use language or multimodal cues to recover latent evidence under coarse supervision~\cite{shen2021dwsa,jin2023ground_articles,huang2023wstsg,li2023boostingwtal,hu2024mmplateau,li2024fps}. Procedure-learning work similarly studies weak instructional structure through planning, step discovery, or collaborative procedure-aware learning~\cite{coin2020,p3iv2022,dvornik2023stepformer,li2024collaborative_weak_video,liu2024llm_alignment}. Text-guided aggregation is also common in video-language modeling: X-Pool replaces uniform pooling with cross-modal weighting, ProST emphasizes partial spatio-temporal matching, and STAN revisits temporal modeling for CLIP-style image-to-video transfer~\cite{gorti2022xpool,li2023prost,liu2023stan}. SurgAlign draws on these ideas, but applies them in a narrower way: it changes how weak positive pairs are constructed during pretraining, without adding a temporal grounding head or a query-conditioned inference pipeline.

The two components of SurgAlign follow directly from this literature. First, temporal grounding methods commonly reason over candidate temporal neighborhoods rather than a single fixed clip: 2D-TAN and MS-2D-TAN represent many adjacent candidate moments with different start/end times, and Moment-DETR detects query-relevant moments and highlights from video using natural language~\cite{twodtan2020,ms2dtan2022,momentdetr2021}. This supports our local expansion step, which treats a weak timestamp as an anchor for a small candidate window rather than as an exact boundary. Second, text-conditioned pooling and token selection methods show that not all visual tokens should contribute equally to a text-video match. X-Pool highlights frames relevant to a given text query, TS2-Net selects informative spatial-temporal tokens, and ProST models partial spatio-temporal correspondence between text and video~\cite{gorti2022xpool,ts2net2022,li2023prost}. This supports our re-selection step, which softly weights candidate frames during training to suppress noisy visual evidence introduced by expansion.

\subsection{Surgical Video-Language Pretraining}

Surgical video understanding has long been constrained by expensive dense annotations for phases, tools, actions, and workflow events~\cite{tecno2020,lessismore2022,rendezvous2022}. Surgical VLP addresses this bottleneck by learning transferable representations from weakly paired video-text data. SurgVLP showed that surgical lecture videos and narrations can support CLIP-style pretraining and zero-shot transfer in the surgical domain~\cite{lecturevideo2023}. Subsequent systems, including HecVL, PeskaVLP, OphCLIP, and SurgLaVi/SurgCLIP, advanced this line through hierarchical supervision, retrieval augmentation, stronger curation, and larger surgical corpora~\cite{hecvl2024,peskavlp2024,ophclip2025,surglavi2025}. These methods establish the value of surgical VLP, but mainly emphasize corpus construction, hierarchy-aware training, domain-specific initialization, or richer pretraining objectives.

Our work is complementary to this surgical VLP line. Instead of proposing a new surgical corpus or a new retrieval architecture, we study the construction of the positive visual evidence inside each weak pair. SurgAlign treats the timestamped interval as a noisy temporal anchor, expands it into a local candidate window, and re-selects caption-relevant frames during training. This makes the method orthogonal to prior advances in data scale and model design: once a weak surgical pair exists, the central question is which local frames should act as positives for contrastive learning.
