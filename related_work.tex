\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Weakly Aligned Video-Language Learning}

Large-scale video-language pretraining often relies on narrations, transcriptions, or automatically produced captions rather than precisely trimmed clips~\cite{miech2019howto100m,pan2022hdvila,wang2024panda70m,vid2seq2023}. In instructional video, several works explicitly exploit this weak synchronization to learn procedure-aware or narration-aware representations~\cite{alayrac2015unsupervised,xu2023procedure_aware_repr,zhou2023procedure_aware_pretraining}. These works establish that weak pairs are useful at scale, but they typically do not ask how a semantically valid interval should be consumed when its decisive visual evidence is temporally sparse or locally shifted. Our paper focuses on that intra-pair supervision problem.

\subsection{Weak Temporal Supervision and Evidence Selection}

Our framing is related to multiple-instance learning, where a positive bag may contain only a subset of truly positive instances~\cite{dietterich1997mil,ilse2018attentionmil}. In weakly supervised temporal alignment and localization, researchers use language or multimodal cues to recover latent evidence under coarse temporal supervision~\cite{shen2021dwsa,jin2023ground_articles,li2023boostingwtal,huang2023wstsg,hu2024mmplateau,li2024fps}. Text-guided aggregation is also common in video-language modeling: X-Pool replaces uniform pooling with cross-modal weighting, ProST emphasizes local spatio-temporal evidence, and STAN revisits temporal modeling for CLIP-style transfer~\cite{gorti2022xpool,li2023prost,liu2023stan}. SurgAlign draws on these ideas, but applies them in a narrower way: it changes only the training rule for weak positive pairs and keeps inference unchanged.

\subsection{Surgical Video-Language Pretraining}

Surgical video understanding has long been constrained by expensive dense annotations for phases, tools, actions, and workflow events~\cite{tecno2020,lessismore2022,rendezvous2022}. Surgical VLP addresses this bottleneck by learning transferable representations from weakly paired video-text data. Early work such as SurgVLP showed that lecture videos and narrations can support CLIP-style pretraining in the surgical domain~\cite{lecturevideo2023}. Subsequent systems, including HecVL, PeskaVLP, OphCLIP, and SurgLaVi, advanced this line through stronger curation, hierarchy-aware supervision, and larger surgical corpora~\cite{hecvl2024,peskavlp2024,ophclip2024,surglavi2025}. These methods establish the value of surgical VLP, but mainly emphasize better corpora, stronger initialization, or richer supervision structures.

Hierarchy is used differently in our paper than in prior hierarchical VLP. Works such as HecVL, HiTeA, and HiVLP suggest that alignment behavior changes across temporal scales~\cite{hecvl2024,ye2023hitea,shao2023hivlp}. We build on that insight, but use hierarchy in a much narrower way: to control how sharp the frame weighting should be and to add a light cross-level regularizer, rather than to build a separate retrieval head or embedding design. Our main question is orthogonal: once a weak surgical pair exists, which local frames should act as positives during training?
