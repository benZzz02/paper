\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Surgical Vision-Language Pretraining}

Surgical video understanding has long been constrained by expensive dense annotations for phases, tools, actions, and workflow events~\cite{tecno2020,lessismore2022,rendezvous2022}. Surgical VLP addresses this bottleneck by learning transferable representations from weakly paired video-text data. Early work such as SurgVLP demonstrated that lecture videos and narrations can support CLIP-style pretraining in the surgical domain~\cite{lecturevideo2023}. Subsequent systems, including HecVL, PeskaVLP, OphCLIP, and SurgLaVi, improved this line through stronger curation, hierarchy-aware supervision, and larger surgical corpora~\cite{hecvl2024,peskavlp2024,ophclip2024,surglavi2025}. These methods establish the value of surgical VLP, but mainly emphasize better corpora, stronger initialization, or richer supervision structures. Our work instead focuses on how weak temporal supervision should be used once a video-text pair has already been formed.

\subsection{Weak Alignment and Temporal Misalignment}

Weakly paired text does not guarantee precise temporal alignment. This issue is already visible inside surgical VLP itself: SurgVLP discusses semantic mismatch between clips and text, SurgLaVi highlights temporal misalignment and commentary drift, and PeskaVLP emphasizes procedural alignment under weak supervision~\cite{lecturevideo2023,surglavi2025,peskavlp2024}. Similar observations have been made in narrated and instructional video. DWSA studies weak sequence alignment when visual actions and language instructions are temporally offset~\cite{shen2021dwsa}. Grounding instructional articles through narrations likewise treats language and video as only weakly synchronized views~\cite{jin2023ground_articles}, and procedure-aware representation learning from narrations shows that spoken descriptions contain useful but imperfectly aligned supervision~\cite{xu2023procedure_aware_repr}. These works support our problem definition: in transcript-driven surgical supervision, a weak pair can be semantically valid while its strongest visual evidence is locally shifted in time.

\subsection{Local Evidence Aggregation and Hierarchical Priors}

Our method is also related to work that replaces uniform temporal pooling with evidence-aware aggregation. Multiple-instance learning offers the basic lens: a positive bag can contain only a subset of truly positive instances~\cite{dietterich1997mil,ilse2018attentionmil}. In weakly supervised temporal localization, language-guided weighting is often used to suppress irrelevant snippets and recover latent evidence under coarse supervision~\cite{li2023boostingwtal,hu2024mmplateau,li2024fps}. In video-language modeling, X-Pool shows that text-conditioned weighting can improve matching over fixed average pooling~\cite{gorti2022xpool}, while ProST emphasizes local spatio-temporal evidence rather than uniform clip-level correspondence~\cite{li2023prost}. STAN further highlights temporal reasoning as a central challenge when adapting CLIP-style pretraining to video~\cite{liu2023stan}. Our work draws on these ideas, but adapts them to surgical weak supervision in a lighter form: expanded temporal evidence and text-guided local reweighting are used strictly as training rules rather than as inference-time retrieval modules.

Hierarchy in our paper is used as a granularity prior rather than as a separate prediction target. Fine-, mid-, and coarse-level captions correspond to different temporal scopes, and prior multi-scale video-language work suggests that alignment behaves differently across such scopes~\cite{ye2023hitea,shao2023hivlp,dvornik2023stepformer,zhou2023procedure_aware_pretraining}. We adopt this insight in a restrained way: fine captions should induce sharper evidence concentration, whereas broader captions should allow smoother weighting. This keeps SurgAlign lightweight while still making use of hierarchical supervision.
