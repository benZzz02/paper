\begin{abstract}
Weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining is a promising route for zero-shot surgical understanding, but most CLIP-style pipelines still align a caption with a pooled embedding from a timestamped interval as if all sampled frames inside that interval were equally reliable positives. In surgical videos, this assumption is often too strong: the visual evidence that supports a caption may only occupy a subset of local frames and can be slightly shifted with respect to the annotated temporal boundary. Hierarchical annotations are also often flattened into ordinary clip-caption pairs, leaving fine-mid-coarse relations underused during training. We address these issues with a train-time window denoising framework with hierarchical anchoring. During training, each weak interval is treated as a noisy temporal anchor, expanded into a small local neighborhood, and softly reweighted by the paired text to produce a denoised visual training view. We additionally structure each mixed-level batch to contain at least one nested fine-mid-coarse triplet and reuse the fine caption as a local semantic anchor for regularizing denoised mid/coarse representations, without adding extra text-encoder passes. Test-time inference remains standard CLIP-style independent encoding of video and text. This design directly targets temporal imprecision and cross-level supervision underuse while preserving the simplicity and scalability of standard dual-encoder inference.

\end{abstract}
