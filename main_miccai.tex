\begin{abstract}
Weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining is a promising route for zero-shot surgical understanding, but most CLIP-style pipelines still align a caption with a pooled embedding from a timestamped interval as if all sampled frames inside that interval were equally reliable positives. In surgical videos, this assumption is often too strong: the visual evidence that supports a caption may only occupy a subset of local frames and can be slightly shifted with respect to the annotated temporal boundary. We address this issue with a train-time window denoising framework that serves as a general training strategy for CLIP-style surgical VLP. During training, each weak interval is treated as a noisy temporal anchor, expanded into a bounded local neighborhood, and softly reweighted by the paired text to produce a denoised visual training view. Fine/mid/coarse annotation levels are used only as evidence-granularity priors, enabling sharper frame selection for fine captions and smoother selection for broader captions. We validate the framework first on a controlled CLIP baseline built from a surgery-domain-pretrained visual backbone, and then on public surgical VLP initializations retrained on SurgLaVi under a unified protocol. Test-time inference remains standard CLIP-style independent encoding of video and text. This design directly targets temporal imprecision while preserving the simplicity and scalability of standard dual-encoder inference.

\end{abstract}
