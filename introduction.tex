\section{Introduction}

Large-scale video-language pretraining increasingly relies on narrated or weakly paired video-text data because precise temporal annotation does not scale~\cite{miech2019howto100m,pan2022hdvila,xu2023procedure_aware_repr,zhou2023procedure_aware_pretraining}. This supervision is attractive precisely because it is abundant, but it is also temporally imprecise: a paired sentence can be semantically correct for a video interval while the frames that visually justify it occupy only a short subsegment. Surgical video is a particularly stringent instance of this regime. Narrations, transcripts, and coarse temporal notes are easier to collect than dense labels, yet decisive evidence often lies in brief tool-tissue interactions embedded in repetitive procedural context~\cite{lecturevideo2023,peskavlp2024,surglavi2025}.

Most CLIP-style training pipelines ignore this structure. They sample frames from the weak interval and pool them into a single positive clip, implicitly assuming that all sampled frames support the paired text to a similar degree. That simplification can be acceptable when alignment noise is small or when broad scene context is sufficient. It is much less appropriate when positive evidence is sparse, locally shifted, or diluted by before/after context. In such cases, the model is asked to align text not with the evidence that justifies it, but with an averaged clip that mixes relevant and irrelevant content.

We study this failure mode as \emph{temporal misalignment within weak video-text pairs}. The mismatch is not between video and text at the pair level; it is between interval-level supervision and frame-level evidence. We argue that weak video-text pairs should not be treated as uniform positives. Instead, a weak interval should be treated as a noisy temporal anchor: semantically useful at the pair level, but only approximate at the frame level. This framing is related to multiple-instance learning and weak temporal localization, where a positive bag may contain only a subset of truly supporting instances~\cite{dietterich1997mil,ilse2018attentionmil,shen2021dwsa,li2023boostingwtal,hu2024mmplateau,li2024fps}. Our goal is narrower than full temporal grounding. We ask whether a video-language model can learn a better training signal by searching a bounded neighborhood around a weak interval and softly emphasizing the frames that best match the paired text.

To test this hypothesis, we introduce \textbf{SurgAlign}, a training-time framework for learning from temporally misaligned weak surgical video-text pairs. SurgAlign expands each weak interval into a bounded temporal neighborhood, samples candidate frames from that neighborhood, and uses the paired text to compute soft frame weights for a local visual view. This local branch is optimized jointly with a standard global branch, so the model preserves stable clip-level alignment while learning to recover sharper evidence from noisy positives. Hierarchical caption levels act only as priors on how concentrated the local weighting should be: fine captions encourage sharp selection, while coarse captions allow smoother aggregation. Test-time inference remains standard CLIP-style video and text encoding.

We evaluate SurgAlign in a matched surgical-domain setting, where data, encoders, and optimization are fixed and only the training rule changes. This matched comparison is important: the paper is not about building a larger surgical VLP system, but about whether better positive-pair construction helps under weak temporal supervision. SurgAlign improves the matched baseline on several zero-shot benchmarks, with the clearest gain on GraSP Step ($+5.43$ Acc / $+5.27$ F1) and additional gains on Cholec80, HeiChole, GraSP Phase, and SARRARP50 Action. At the same time, the gains are not uniform across all tasks, which helps define the scope of the method.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item We identify a supervision mismatch in weak surgical VLP: a video-text pair can be semantically correct at the interval level while the visual evidence that justifies it is sparse, local, and temporally shifted at the frame level.
    \item We propose SurgAlign, a training-time framework that treats weak intervals as noisy temporal anchors through bounded temporal expansion, text-guided local reweighting, and hierarchy-aware selection priors, while keeping inference unchanged.
    \item We show in matched zero-shot surgical evaluation that SurgAlign improves a matched baseline most clearly on evidence-sensitive tasks, supporting the view that better positive-pair construction matters under weak temporal supervision.
\end{enumerate}
