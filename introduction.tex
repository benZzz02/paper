\section{Introduction}

Understanding surgical videos is important for a wide range of downstream applications, including workflow recognition, context-aware assistance, retrospective procedure analysis, surgical training, and automatic indexing of operative videos. However, traditional supervised learning in this domain depends heavily on dense expert annotations for phases, steps, tools, actions, and triplets, which are expensive to obtain, difficult to standardize across centers, and hard to scale to diverse procedures. These limitations make it difficult to build task-specific models for every workflow label space and every surgical domain, and they motivate learning transferable representations that can support zero-shot or low-supervision surgical evaluation. In particular, zero-shot surgical evaluation requires representations that capture semantically meaningful visual concepts beyond a fixed closed-set label space, so that knowledge learned from weak supervision can transfer across procedures, centers, and downstream tasks.

Against this background, surgical video-language pretraining (VLP) has emerged as a promising paradigm. Existing studies have shown that lecture videos, narrated procedures, and automatically constructed clip-caption pairs can provide scalable weak supervision for learning transferable workflow representations. Compared with training directly on task-specific labels, VLP offers a more scalable route to connect visual patterns with surgical semantics expressed in natural language, making it attractive for zero-shot transfer to phase, step, action, tool, and triplet recognition. More recent resources such as SurgLaVi further improve the scale, hierarchy, and curation quality of weakly supervised surgical video-text data, making CLIP-style pretraining increasingly practical for surgical understanding.

However, scalable weak supervision does not imply precise alignment. In most current CLIP-style surgical VLP pipelines, a weakly paired sample is constructed by associating a caption with a timestamped video interval, sampling multiple frames from that interval, aggregating them into a single clip embedding, and aligning that clip embedding with the caption through a standard contrastive objective. This formulation is simple and effective, but it also imposes a strong assumption: the sampled interval is treated as if it provided a uniformly reliable positive visual representation for the paired text.

That assumption is often too strong in surgical videos. Even when a weak clip-caption pair is globally valid, the visual evidence that directly supports the text is frequently concentrated in only a subset of local frames and may be slightly shifted relative to the annotated timestamp. This issue is particularly acute in surgery: transitions between phases are subtle, actions often unfold over short local spans, appearance differences across neighboring steps can be small, and textual descriptions are not always synchronized with the most informative visual moments. As a result, treating the entire sampled interval as a homogeneous positive sample can dilute the supervision signal and encourage alignment to contextual, transitional, or only weakly related content.

This mismatch matters because the downstream objectives of surgical VLP are not purely coarse-grained. Zero-shot phase recognition can sometimes benefit from broad contextual cues, but zero-shot step, action, tool, and especially triplet recognition require the model to associate text with more specific local evidence, such as short-lived instrument--action--target interactions. If weak supervision is consumed through uniformly pooled interval-level positives, the learned embedding may overemphasize procedure context and underemphasize the discriminative local evidence needed for fine-grained transfer. In other words, the problem is not only annotation noise; it is that the current training assumption can systematically bias the representation toward coarse workflow priors rather than the local surgical semantics that zero-shot evaluation ultimately demands.

SurgLaVi-style hierarchy reveals a second unresolved issue. Fine-, mid-, and coarse-level annotations are not merely three independent caption pools; they encode nested temporal relations and different semantic granularities. Yet in many CLIP-style training pipelines, hierarchical captions are still consumed mainly as additional weak pairs once they have been flattened into a shared training set. This underuses the most distinctive part of hierarchical supervision: fine captions are temporally sharper and semantically more local, while mid/coarse captions summarize broader procedural context. A training pipeline that ignores these cross-level relations leaves potentially useful supervision on the table.

These observations suggest a more precise formulation of the problem. The first issue is 
\emph{intra-sample temporal imprecision}: a weak video-text pair may be semantically correct in the large while still containing only a limited subset of genuinely supportive frames, and these frames may lie slightly before or after the annotated temporal boundary. The second issue is \emph{inter-level relation underuse}: hierarchical annotations should not only increase sample count, but also provide structured supervision across temporal granularities. From this perspective, the key modeling question is no longer only whether a caption matches an interval globally, but also how to denoise temporally imprecise positives and how to turn hierarchical annotations into explicit training-time constraints.

This way of formulating the problem is not ad hoc. Recent work on weakly supervised temporal localization and multiple-instance learning argues that coarse supervision should be treated as a partially positive bag rather than a uniformly reliable unit, and that latent evidence can be recovered through soft relevance weighting~\cite{dietterich1997mil,ilse2018attentionmil,li2023boostingwtal,hu2024mmplateau,li2024fps}. Text-conditioned video aggregation methods such as X-Pool show that fixed mean pooling can encode irrelevant visual content and that frame contributions can instead be reweighted according to cross-modal relevance~\cite{gorti2022xpool}. On the hierarchy side, temporal-aware and hierarchical VLP methods such as HiTeA and HecVL indicate that preserving multi-granularity structure can provide stronger supervision than treating all temporal scales independently~\cite{ye2023hitea,hecvl2024}. Our method adapts these ideas to surgical VLP in a deliberately lightweight way.

Motivated by this view, we study weakly supervised surgical VLP through the lens of \emph{train-time temporal denoising with hierarchical set alignment}. First, instead of trusting the annotated interval uniformly, we expand it into a small local neighborhood and let the paired text softly reweight candidate frames inside the expanded window. This produces a denoised selected view that is trained jointly with a standard global view. Second, instead of using hierarchy only as a passive metadata field, we model it as a set-valued supervision structure. Each mixed-level batch is organized to expose multiple valid fine-mid-coarse relations, and every mid/coarse parent is regularized against the set of finer-grained captions nested inside it. In this way, fine captions act not as a single reused anchor, but as local semantic atoms whose weighted set defines what the enclosing parent representation should preserve.

This design complements rather than replaces existing surgical VLP pipelines. We do not argue against hierarchical supervision, stronger backbones, or larger datasets. Instead, we argue that even under high-quality weak supervision, the way temporally noisy positives and hierarchical relations are consumed during training still matters. Our goal is therefore to improve how weak supervision is trusted and structured: from uniform interval-level pooling to train-time window denoising with structured cross-level anchoring, while preserving standard CLIP-style test-time inference.

In summary, this work makes three contributions. First, we identify \emph{temporal imprecision inside weakly paired intervals} and \emph{hierarchical relation underuse} as two central and under-modeled issues in surgical VLP. Second, we propose a train-time window denoising framework that expands each weak interval into a local neighborhood and uses paired text to softly reweight candidate frames, thereby constructing a denoised visual training view without changing the test-time inference path. Third, we show how hierarchical annotations can be converted from a data organization property into a supervision structure through hierarchical set alignment, where mid/coarse representations are regularized against weighted sets of nested fine captions rather than isolated single anchors.
