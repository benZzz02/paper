\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{L}{arge}-scale video-language pretraining increasingly relies on narrated or otherwise weakly paired video-text data because precise temporal annotation does not scale~\cite{miech2019howto100m,pan2022hdvila,xu2023procedure_aware_repr,zhou2023procedure_aware_pretraining}. This supervision is abundant, but it is also temporally imprecise: a paired sentence can be semantically correct for a video interval even though the frames that visually justify it occupy only a short subsegment. Surgical video is a particularly difficult instance of this regime. Narrations, transcripts, and coarse temporal notes are much easier to collect than dense labels, yet decisive evidence often lies in brief tool-tissue interactions embedded in repetitive procedural context~\cite{lecturevideo2023,peskavlp2024,surglavi2025}.

Most CLIP-style training pipelines ignore this structure. They sample frames from the weak interval and pool them into a single positive clip, implicitly assuming that all sampled frames support the paired text to a similar degree. That assumption can be acceptable when alignment noise is small or when broad scene context is enough. It is much less appropriate when the relevant evidence is sparse, slightly shifted, or diluted by before/after context. In that setting, the model aligns the text not with the frames that justify it, but with an average that mixes relevant and irrelevant content.

We study this failure mode as \emph{temporal misalignment within weak video-text pairs}. The mismatch is not between video and text at the pair level; it is between interval-level supervision and frame-level evidence. We therefore argue that weak video-text pairs should not be treated as uniform positives. A weak interval is useful for locating a region of interest, but it is too coarse to define a clean positive clip on its own. This view is related to multiple-instance learning and weak temporal localization, where a positive bag may contain only a subset of truly supportive instances~\cite{dietterich1997mil,ilse2018attentionmil,shen2021dwsa,li2023boostingwtal,hu2024mmplateau,li2024fps}. Our goal is narrower than full temporal grounding: we ask whether a video-language model can learn a better positive signal by searching a small neighborhood around a weak interval and emphasizing the frames that best match the paired text.

Figure~\ref{fig:problem_setup} illustrates the core supervision mismatch. The weak interval can be semantically correct at the pair level while still containing only a small amount of frame-level evidence that truly justifies the paired text. Uniformly pooling such frames dilutes the positive signal with temporally nearby but weakly relevant context.

To test this hypothesis, we introduce \textbf{SurgAlign}, a simple training-time method for learning from temporally misaligned weak surgical video-text pairs. SurgAlign expands each weak interval into a bounded local window, samples candidate frames from that window, and uses the paired text to assign soft weights to those frames. A standard global branch preserves stable clip-level alignment, while an auxiliary weighted branch encourages the model to focus on sharper local evidence. When hierarchical captions are available, they are used only to control how sharp the frame weights should be and to lightly regularize nearby levels. Test-time inference remains standard CLIP-style video and text encoding.

We evaluate SurgAlign in a matched surgical-domain setting, where data, encoders, temporal modeling, and optimization are fixed and only the training rule changes. This comparison isolates the question of interest: whether better positive-pair construction helps under weak temporal supervision. SurgAlign improves the matched baseline on several zero-shot benchmarks, with the clearest gain on GraSP Step ($+5.43$ Acc / $+5.27$ F1) and additional gains on Cholec80, HeiChole, GraSP Phase, and SARRARP50 Action. The gains are not uniform across all tasks, which helps define the scope of the method.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We identify a supervision mismatch in weak surgical VLP: a video-text pair can be semantically correct at the interval level while the visual evidence that justifies it is sparse, local, and temporally shifted at the frame level.
    \item We propose SurgAlign, a training-time method that treats weak intervals as noisy temporal anchors through local window expansion and text-guided frame weighting, while using hierarchy only to control weighting sharpness and lightly regularize nearby levels.
    \item We show in matched zero-shot surgical evaluation that SurgAlign helps most on evidence-sensitive tasks, supporting the view that better positive-pair construction matters under weak temporal supervision.
\end{enumerate}
