\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

SurgAlign is a training-time refinement for weak surgical VLP. It changes only how positive video-text pairs are consumed during training. The method is motivated by one observation: in weak surgical pairs, supervision is often reliable at the interval level but noisy at the frame level. Instead of assuming that every frame inside a weak interval is equally positive, SurgAlign treats the interval as an approximate temporal anchor and recovers a better local visual view around it. The method has three components: bounded temporal expansion, text-guided local weighting, and joint optimization with a standard global branch. The video and text encoders, as well as the test-time pipeline, remain unchanged.

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:method_setup}

Each training sample is written as
\begin{equation}
(V, [t_s, t_e], y, \ell),
\end{equation}
where $V$ is a surgical video, $[t_s, t_e]$ is the weak temporal interval, $y$ is the paired text, and $\ell \in \{f, m, c\}$ denotes the fine, mid, or coarse annotation level. In a standard CLIP-style baseline, $M$ frames are sampled from $[t_s, t_e]$, encoded by a visual backbone $E_v$, aggregated into one clip representation, and aligned with a text representation produced by a text encoder $E_t$. If $A_v(\cdot)$ is the temporal aggregation head and $P_v(\cdot), P_t(\cdot)$ are the projection heads, the baseline computes
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{glob}} = \operatorname{norm}\!\left(P_v(A_v(E_v(c)))\right), \qquad
u = E_t(y), \qquad
t = \operatorname{norm}\!\left(P_t(u)\right),
\end{equation}
and optimizes a symmetric contrastive loss.

This formulation is effective when the sampled clip is already well aligned with the text. Our setting is weaker: the pair can be semantically valid even when the truly supportive visual evidence occupies only a subset of frames. In other words, the baseline uses one positive label for a set of frames that do not equally support the paired text. SurgAlign keeps the same encoders and contrastive objective, but changes how the positive visual evidence is collected and aggregated during training.

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:method_overview}

SurgAlign uses two training branches. Starting from a weak temporal interval, it first expands the interval into a bounded neighborhood and samples candidate frames from the expanded window, because the most informative evidence may lie slightly before or after the annotated boundaries. It then computes text-conditioned frame weights and aggregates a selected local visual view, because even within that neighborhood not all frames are equally evidential. Training combines:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item a global branch that preserves stable interval-level video-text alignment; and
    \item a selected branch that emphasizes text-relevant evidence inside the expanded temporal neighborhood.
\end{enumerate}
When fine, mid, and coarse annotations from the same video are jointly available, SurgAlign can additionally regularize adjacent levels for local compatibility. All extra components are used only during pretraining.

\subsection{Temporal Neighborhood Expansion}
\label{sec:method_expand}

Weak narration timestamps often miss the most informative visual moment by a small temporal offset. We therefore replace the original interval with a bounded expanded interval
\begin{equation}
[t_s', t_e'] = \operatorname{Expand}([t_s, t_e]; \rho),
\end{equation}
where $\rho \geq 1$ is an expansion ratio and the interval is clipped to the valid video duration. We then sample a multi-frame candidate clip
\begin{equation}
c_{\mathrm{exp}} = \{x_1', \ldots, x_M'\}
\end{equation}
from $[t_s', t_e']$.

This expansion is deliberately local. SurgAlign does not attempt full temporal grounding; it only enlarges the search space enough to keep nearby supportive frames available during training when the annotated interval is slightly misaligned.

\subsection{Text-Guided Local Evidence Selection}
\label{sec:method_selection}

Let the visual encoder produce frame tokens
\begin{equation}
\{h_1, \ldots, h_M\} = E_v(c_{\mathrm{exp}}),
\end{equation}
and let the text encoder produce token-level hidden states together with the pooled text feature
\begin{equation}
\{w_1, \ldots, w_L\}, \quad u = E_t(y).
\end{equation}
The projected global text embedding $t = \operatorname{norm}(P_t(u))$ is still used by the contrastive objective. The selected branch instead uses token-level text states to construct local temporal evidence inside the expanded window.

We first build a text query by mean-pooling the valid token states and applying a lightweight projection:
\begin{equation}
\bar{w} = \frac{1}{L'}\sum_{r=1}^{L} m_r w_r, \qquad
q = \operatorname{norm}(Q_t(\bar{w})),
\end{equation}
where $m_r \in \{0,1\}$ is the token mask and $L' = \sum_r m_r$. Each frame token is projected in the same latent space,
\begin{equation}
\tilde{h}_j = \operatorname{norm}(Q_v(h_j)),
\end{equation}
and matched with the text query through cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
s_j = q^\top \tilde{h}_j.
\end{equation}

To inject temporal granularity without introducing separate heads, we use the annotation level $\ell$ only to control \emph{how concentrated} the frame weighting should be. Fine-level captions typically describe narrower events, whereas mid/coarse captions tend to summarize broader context. We therefore use a level-specific selection temperature
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{sel}}^{(\ell)} =
\begin{cases}
\tau_f, & \ell = f,\\
\tau_m, & \ell = m,\\
\tau_c, & \ell = c,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\tau_f < \tau_m < \tau_c$, so fine captions induce sharper temporal selection and broader captions allow smoother weighting.

The frame weights are then computed as
\begin{equation}
\alpha_j = \operatorname{softmax}_{j}\left(\frac{s_j}{\tau_{\mathrm{sel}}^{(\ell)}}\right),
\end{equation}
and the selected visual view is obtained by weighted aggregation,
\begin{equation}
z_{\mathrm{sel}} = \sum_{j=1}^{M} \alpha_j h_j, \qquad
v_{\mathrm{sel}} = \operatorname{norm}(P_v(z_{\mathrm{sel}})).
\end{equation}

The selected branch can be viewed as a denoised auxiliary view of the same weak pair. The global branch still provides stable clip-level supervision, while the selected branch teaches the model to recover text-relevant temporal evidence from imprecise positives.

\subsection{Hierarchical Consistency Across Adjacent Levels}
\label{sec:method_consistency}

When fine, mid, and coarse samples from the same video are jointly observed during training, their selected views should remain compatible rather than drifting independently in the embedding space. We therefore construct mixed-level batches and, when triplet anchoring is enabled, include same-video nested triplets satisfying
\begin{equation}
[t_s^{f}, t_e^{f}] \subseteq [t_s^{m}, t_e^{m}] \subseteq [t_s^{c}, t_e^{c}].
\end{equation}
For the available triplets $\mathcal{T}$ in a batch, we apply an adjacent-level consistency loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{hier}} =
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}
\sum_{(f,m,c)\in \mathcal{T}}
\Big[
1 - \operatorname{cos}(v_{\mathrm{sel}}^f, v_{\mathrm{sel}}^m) +
1 - \operatorname{cos}(v_{\mathrm{sel}}^m, v_{\mathrm{sel}}^c)
\Big].
\end{equation}
We regularize only adjacent levels, not all pairs, because the goal is not to collapse all temporal scopes into a single representation. The role of this term is simply to discourage incompatible local evidence across nested supervision.

\subsection{Training Objective and Inference}
\label{sec:method_objective}

The global branch produces
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{glob}} = \operatorname{norm}(P_v(A_v(E_v(c_{\mathrm{exp}})))).
\end{equation}
Both training branches share the same global text embedding $t$ and differ only in how the expanded clip is aggregated on the video side. Using $t$, we optimize two symmetric CLIP-style contrastive objectives:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{glob}} =
\mathcal{L}_{v \rightarrow t}(v_{\mathrm{glob}}, t) +
\mathcal{L}_{t \rightarrow v}(t, v_{\mathrm{glob}}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sel}} =
\mathcal{L}_{v \rightarrow t}(v_{\mathrm{sel}}, t) +
\mathcal{L}_{t \rightarrow v}(t, v_{\mathrm{sel}}).
\end{equation}
The final objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} =
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{glob}}
 + \lambda_{\mathrm{sel}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sel}}
 + \lambda_{\mathrm{hier}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{hier}}.
\end{equation}

Here, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{glob}}$ preserves stable clip-level alignment, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sel}}$ teaches the model to recover local evidence through text-guided reweighting, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{hier}}$ stabilizes the selected branch across neighboring annotation granularities.

At inference time, SurgAlign falls back to standard CLIP-style encoding:
\begin{equation}
v = \operatorname{encode\_image}(c), \qquad
t = \operatorname{encode\_text}(y),
\end{equation}
followed by cosine similarity in the shared embedding space. No expanded window, frame reweighting, or hierarchy-specific matching is required at test time. This separation is intentional: SurgAlign is a method for improving weak supervision during pretraining, not a query-conditioned inference system.
