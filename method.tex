\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our goal is to improve how weak surgical video-text supervision is used without replacing the underlying CLIP-style backbone. The key observation is that a weakly paired sample should not be treated as a uniformly reliable positive unit. Instead, the local visual evidence supporting the paired caption may occupy only a subset of the annotated interval and may be slightly shifted in time. We therefore formulate weakly supervised surgical VLP as \emph{train-time window denoising}: training explicitly denoises temporally imprecise positives within each sample, while test-time inference remains standard CLIP. Because the method only changes how training frames are aggregated before contrastive alignment, it can be applied to any CLIP-style surgical VLP pipeline trained on timestamped interval-caption pairs.

Conceptually, our design is related to multiple-instance learning and text-guided weak temporal localization. A weak interval is closer to a partially positive bag than to a uniformly positive set of frames~\cite{dietterich1997mil,ilse2018attentionmil,li2023boostingwtal,hu2024mmplateau,li2024fps}. We also use fine/mid/coarse annotation levels as a lightweight evidence-granularity prior, since broader captions naturally support smoother temporal evidence than short local captions. All additional mechanisms are used only during training, and test-time inference remains unchanged.

\subsection{Method Overview}
\label{sec:method_overview}

Our framework follows the same high-level dual-encoder paradigm as CLIP-style surgical VLP, but changes how temporally noisy positives are consumed during training. Starting from a weakly annotated interval, we first expand it into a small local temporal neighborhood and sample an expanded candidate clip. We then compute frame-text alignment scores inside this expanded window and transform them into soft frame weights. These weights define a denoised visual view that is trained jointly with a standard global visual view derived from the same expanded clip. In our experiments, we instantiate this rule in two settings: a CLIP model built from a surgery-domain-pretrained visual backbone, and public surgical VLP initializations retrained on SurgLaVi.

Fine/mid/coarse annotation levels are used only to modulate the sharpness of this frame selection process. Fine captions correspond to more localized evidence and therefore encourage sharper concentration, whereas broader captions are allowed to retain smoother evidence distributions. During inference, however, we discard all training-only denoising logic and use standard CLIP-style video and text encoders exactly as usual.

\subsection{Problem Setup and CLIP Baseline}
\label{sec:method_setup}

Each training sample is written as
\begin{equation}
(V, [t_s, t_e], y, \ell),
\end{equation}
where $V$ denotes a surgical video, $[t_s, t_e]$ is the annotated time interval, $y$ is the paired caption, and $\ell \in \{f,m,c\}$ indicates whether the caption comes from the fine, mid, or coarse annotation level. In our final formulation, the level label is used only as an evidence-granularity prior for denoising; we do not add a separate cross-level loss.

Let $E_v(\cdot)$ and $E_t(\cdot)$ denote the visual and textual backbones, let $A_v(\cdot)$ denote the temporal aggregation head, and let $P_v(\cdot)$ and $P_t(\cdot)$ denote the projection heads. In the baseline, frames are sampled from the annotated interval to form a multi-frame clip
\begin{equation}
c = \{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_M\},
\end{equation}
from which per-frame features are extracted as
\begin{equation}
h_j = E_v(x_j), \qquad j=1,\dots,M.
\end{equation}
These frame features are temporally aggregated into a clip representation
\begin{equation}
h_{\text{base}} = A_v(h_1, h_2, \dots, h_M),
\end{equation}
and projected into the shared embedding space as
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{base}} = \operatorname{norm}(P_v(h_{\text{base}})), \qquad
t = \operatorname{norm}(P_t(E_t(y))).
\end{equation}
Standard CLIP-style training then optimizes a symmetric InfoNCE objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{base}} =
\mathcal{L}_{v \rightarrow t}(v_{\text{base}}, t) +
\mathcal{L}_{t \rightarrow v}(t, v_{\text{base}}).
\end{equation}

This formulation is effective but implicitly assumes that the sampled interval provides a uniformly reliable positive visual representation for the paired text. Our method relaxes this assumption without replacing the underlying CLIP-style architecture, which is precisely why it can be reused across different surgical VLP initializations.

\subsection{Train-Time Local Temporal Expansion}
\label{sec:method_expand}

Because weak timestamps may be locally imprecise, we first expand the annotated interval into a bounded local neighborhood during training:
\begin{equation}
[t_s', t_e'] = \operatorname{Expand}([t_s, t_e]; \rho),
\end{equation}
where $\rho \geq 1$ is a window expansion ratio and the expanded interval is clipped to the valid video duration. We then sample a multi-frame clip
\begin{equation}
c_{\text{exp}} = \{x'_1, x'_2, \dots, x'_M\}
\end{equation}
from the expanded interval using the same frame sampling budget as the baseline.

The expanded clip is encoded by the same visual backbone and temporal head:
\begin{equation}
z_{\text{exp}}, \{h'_1,\dots,h'_M\} = A_v(E_v(c_{\text{exp}})),
\end{equation}
where $z_{\text{exp}}$ is the global pooled feature and $h'_j$ are frame-level tokens. The global visual embedding used by the main branch is
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{base}} = \operatorname{norm}(P_v(z_{\text{exp}})).
\end{equation}

The purpose of expansion is not to solve full temporal grounding. It is simply to expose a slightly wider temporal neighborhood around a noisy timestamp so that supportive frames near the interval boundary are still available during training.

\subsection{Text-Guided Soft Frame Selection}
\label{sec:method_selection}

We next estimate how much each frame in the expanded clip supports the paired caption. Let
\begin{equation}
\{w_1,\dots,w_L\} = E_t(y)
\end{equation}
denote the token features of the paired caption. We introduce lightweight local projection heads $Q_v(\cdot)$ and $Q_t(\cdot)$ for frame-token alignment, and compute a frame-token similarity map
\begin{equation}
A_{j\ell} = \operatorname{sim}(Q_v(h'_j), Q_t(w_\ell)) / \tau_{\text{loc}},
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\text{loc}}$ is a local alignment temperature. For each frame, we keep the strongest token evidence:
\begin{equation}
s_j = \max_{\ell} A_{j\ell}.
\end{equation}
This reflects the intuition that a frame should be considered informative if it strongly matches at least one salient textual cue, rather than needing to match the entire caption uniformly.

The frame scores are converted into a soft selection distribution
\begin{equation}
\alpha_j = \frac{\exp(s_j / \tau^{(\ell)}_{\text{sel}})}{\sum_{k=1}^{M} \exp(s_k / \tau^{(\ell)}_{\text{sel}})},
\end{equation}
where $\tau^{(\ell)}_{\text{sel}}$ is a level-dependent selection temperature defined below. The resulting denoised visual view is constructed by reweighting the frame tokens:
\begin{equation}
z_{\text{sel}} = \sum_{j=1}^{M} \alpha_j h'_j,
\qquad
v_{\text{sel}} = \operatorname{norm}(P_v(z_{\text{sel}})).
\end{equation}

This is a soft frame reweighting mechanism, not a second inference branch. Both $v_{\text{base}}$ and $v_{\text{sel}}$ come from the same expanded clip, so the selected view can be interpreted as a denoised auxiliary view of the same weak pair.

\subsection{Level-Aware Evidence Granularity}
\label{sec:method_granularity}

The hierarchical annotations in SurgLaVi-style data correspond to different temporal granularities. Fine-level captions typically describe shorter and more localized actions, whereas mid- and coarse-level captions summarize broader temporal segments. We therefore let the expected concentration of frame evidence vary by annotation level through a level-dependent selection temperature:
\begin{equation}
\tau^{(\ell)}_{\text{sel}} =
\begin{cases}
\tau_f, & \text{if } \ell=f,\\
\tau_m, & \text{if } \ell=m,\\
\tau_c, & \text{if } \ell=c.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
With $\tau_f < \tau_m < \tau_c$, fine-level supervision encourages sharper frame concentration within a short interval, whereas coarse-level supervision allows smoother evidence distributions over a broader temporal span.

This is the only way hierarchy enters our final formulation. We use the annotation level as a prior on evidence granularity inside the denoising mechanism itself, rather than introducing an additional hierarchy-specific contrastive objective.

\subsection{Training Objective and Test-Time Inference}
\label{sec:method_objective}

The paired text embedding is always computed by the standard text branch:
\begin{equation}
t = \operatorname{norm}(P_t(E_t(y))).
\end{equation}
We optimize two CLIP-style symmetric contrastive terms,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{base}} =
\mathcal{L}_{v \rightarrow t}(v_{\text{base}}, t) +
\mathcal{L}_{t \rightarrow v}(t, v_{\text{base}}),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sel}} =
\mathcal{L}_{v \rightarrow t}(v_{\text{sel}}, t) +
\mathcal{L}_{t \rightarrow v}(t, v_{\text{sel}}).
\end{equation}
The final objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} =
\mathcal{L}_{\text{base}} +
\lambda_{\text{sel}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{sel}},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{sel}}$ controls the strength of the denoised auxiliary view.

This formulation directly targets interval-level temporal imprecision while remaining lightweight. The denoised selected view reduces the effect of temporally noisy positives within each sample, and the level-aware temperature keeps the same denoising rule compatible with fine-, mid-, and coarse-level evidence granularity. Yet test-time evaluation remains standard CLIP-style encoding:
\begin{equation}
v = \operatorname{encode\_image}(c), \qquad t = \operatorname{encode\_text}(y),
\end{equation}
followed by standard similarity scoring. All denoising mechanisms are therefore training-time supervision rules rather than new test-time retrieval procedures.
