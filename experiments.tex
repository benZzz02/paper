\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_data}

We adopt SurgLaVi / SurgLaVi-Beta as the pretraining resource and preserve its weakly supervised training protocol. Each sample consists of a video interval with hierarchical textual annotations. In our implementation, every sample uses its own paired caption for frame selection and contrastive alignment, while the annotation level is used only as a lightweight prior on expected evidence concentration during denoising. When fine-, mid-, and coarse-level annotations are jointly enabled, batches are constructed with a fixed cross-level composition and can additionally contain same-video nested fine-mid-coarse triplets.

Our downstream target is zero-shot surgical workflow recognition. To avoid drawing conclusions from a single coarse task, we evaluate on the broader SurgLaVi-style suite covering phase, step, action, tool, and triplet recognition. This evaluation setting is important for our method because train-time temporal denoising is expected to matter most when downstream recognition depends on fine-grained local semantics rather than only broad workflow context.

Our evaluation follows two complementary settings. The first is a controlled setting built from our own surgery-domain-pretrained visual backbone and a standard CLIP-style text branch. This setting isolates whether temporal denoising is effective when everything except the training rule is held fixed. The second is a broader adaptation setting, where we start from public surgical VLP model initializations and retrain them on SurgLaVi under a unified protocol. In both settings, the baseline samples multiple frames from each annotated interval, aggregates them with a lightweight temporal head, and aligns the resulting clip embedding with the paired caption through bidirectional contrastive learning. Our method keeps the same model family and optimization recipe, but replaces uniform interval-level trust with train-time window denoising. Unless otherwise stated, the current implementation uses the expanded clip for both the global branch and the denoised auxiliary branch, eight frames per training clip, a window expansion ratio of 2.0, a local alignment temperature of 0.07, level-aware selection temperatures of 0.6/0.9/1.2 for fine/mid/coarse supervision, and an auxiliary selection loss weight of 0.5.

The strongest method claim comes from the matched-backbone comparison in the controlled setting. The public-model adaptation experiment then asks a different question: whether the same denoising rule still provides gains when training starts from existing surgical VLP initializations instead of our own backbone construction.

\subsection{Main Benchmark Comparison}
\label{sec:exp_main}

Our primary quantitative evidence is a broad zero-shot comparison against representative surgical VLP baselines and recent stronger pretraining pipelines. Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark_comparison} evaluates our temporally denoised model against CLIP, MedSigLIP, SurgVLP, HecVL, PeskaVLP, SurgCLIP($\beta$), and full SurgCLIP on the common SurgLaVi-style benchmark suite. This comparison is important for our paper because it shows where temporal denoising is practically competitive in the current landscape, especially on tasks that depend more strongly on local temporal evidence.

The pattern is mixed but informative. Our model does not uniformly surpass the strongest full-data baseline, namely SurgCLIP, and we should not claim that it does. However, it remains competitive with or stronger than several prior surgical VLP baselines, and it achieves clear gains over SurgCLIP($\beta$) on StrasBypass70 phase, GraSP phase, GraSP step, and GraSP tool recognition. This task profile is consistent with our method hypothesis: train-time temporal denoising is most useful when transferable evidence is concentrated in a smaller local temporal span and when weak timestamp noise can significantly dilute uniform pooling.

\subsection{Adapting Public Surgical VLP Initializations}

The second experiment tests whether the same denoising rule remains useful beyond our own backbone construction. Here, we start from public surgical VLP model initializations and retrain them on SurgLaVi under a unified protocol. This experiment does not compare against the original reported numbers from each paper, since those differ in data, training schedule, and evaluation details. Instead, for each initialization, we run two matched retraining variants on SurgLaVi: one with standard interval-level alignment and one with our train-time window denoising.

This setting is intentionally narrower than a full method reimplementation. We do not claim to exactly reproduce each published surgical VLP pipeline end-to-end. Rather, we ask a practical transfer question: if a practitioner starts from an existing surgical VLP initialization and retrains on SurgLaVi, does replacing uniform interval pooling with temporal denoising provide a consistent gain?

We also test whether the same train-time denoising rule transfers to a different initialization family. Table~\ref{tab:peskavlp_retrain_validation} compares a PeskaVLP-initialized retraining run with and without our method under the same SurgLaVi retraining setup. Here the evidence is again mixed. The denoised variant improves AutoLaparo phase, BernBypass70 phase, GraSP step, and StrasBypass70 phase, which is consistent with the hypothesis that temporal denoising can help some locally grounded workflow tasks. However, it underperforms the plain retraining run on eight of the twelve reported benchmarks, yielding a slightly negative average delta overall. We therefore view this experiment as a transfer validation rather than a headline result: it shows that the method can provide gains beyond a single initialization family, but it also reinforces that the current formulation remains task-dependent.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}

The ablation study is conducted on the controlled backbone setting, since that setting provides the cleanest isolation of the denoising mechanism. The most important ablation question is not whether the full model is better than a generic literature baseline, but which parts of the train-time temporal denoising pipeline are actually responsible for the gains.

We therefore recommend the following progression:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item matched CLIP-style baseline with uniform clip-text alignment;
    \item baseline + expanded training window;
    \item baseline + text-guided soft frame selection;
    \item baseline + denoised auxiliary contrastive branch;
    \item baseline + level-aware evidence granularity;
    \item baseline + same-video hierarchical batch anchoring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Diagnostic Analysis}
\label{sec:exp_qualitative}

Because the paper is motivated by temporal imprecision in weak intervals, diagnostics should directly test whether the proposed model is selecting more credible frame evidence and whether denoising behaves as intended. We therefore recommend four types of analysis.

\paragraph{Qualitative frame-weight maps.}
Visualize sampled frames within the expanded temporal neighborhood together with their soft selection weights. Representative examples should include both successful denoising cases and failure cases where distracting context remains dominant.

\paragraph{Frame concentration statistics.}
Report summaries such as frame-weight entropy, peak frame weight, and the relative concentration patterns of fine-, mid-, and coarse-level supervision. These quantities are model diagnostics, not standalone claims about the dataset.

\paragraph{Boundary sensitivity.}
Analyze whether the selected view tends to move toward the interior of the expanded window, toward one boundary, or toward the original timestamp center. Such diagnostics help show whether denoising is actually compensating for temporal drift rather than simply sharpening arbitrary frames.

\paragraph{Noise robustness.}
Perturb the weak timestamps in a controlled way and compare how rapidly the matched baseline and the proposed model degrade. Such analysis directly tests whether train-time window denoising is more robust to weak temporal supervision noise than uniform clip-level alignment.
