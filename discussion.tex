\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

SurgAlign is best understood as a supervision refinement method. It addresses one specific weakness of weak surgical VLP: a video-text pair can be semantically valid while only a small subset of frames provides visually supportive evidence. For that reason, the method is intentionally training-only. We do not introduce a new test-time retrieval head or a query-conditioned inference pipeline; the claim is simply that weak temporal positives should be consumed more carefully during pretraining.

The current results also clarify the present limitation. Temporal expansion alone is not the contribution, and it is not enough to guarantee improvement. The useful signal appears when the model learns to place text-conditioned evidence mass beyond the noisy annotated boundary, but the resulting gains are not yet uniformly stable across tasks. In particular, the method assumes that the paired text remains semantically informative and that the relevant temporal offset is local rather than arbitrarily long-range.

This suggests a clear next step for the line of work. Future improvements should focus less on making inference more complex and more on making the denoising process more reliable, for example through stronger local scoring functions, adaptive expansion policies, or partially refined timestamps for direct evidence evaluation. The broader implication is that progress in weakly supervised surgical VLP may depend as much on how supervision is consumed as on the scale of the backbone or corpus.
