\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the proposed method is designed to improve.}
The proposed framework is motivated by two specific failure modes of weak surgical video-text pretraining. First, the paired interval can be globally correct while still containing only a small subset of truly supportive frames. Second, even when hierarchical annotations are available, they can still be underused if fine-, mid-, and coarse-level samples are flattened into ordinary independent pairs. Our method is therefore not primarily intended to replace stronger backbones, better corpora, or richer caption hierarchies. Instead, it improves how existing weak supervision is consumed during training by reducing the impact of temporally imprecise positives and by turning cross-level relations into explicit supervision.

\paragraph{Why training-only denoising is a deliberate design choice.}
A key design principle of this work is to separate \emph{training noise handling} from \emph{test-time inference}. Since the temporal imprecision problem is introduced by the weakly supervised pretraining data, it should be addressed where it occurs: during pretraining. For this reason, we do not carry text-guided frame selection or hierarchical anchoring into downstream inference. Test-time evaluation remains standard CLIP-style independent encoding of video and text, which preserves scalability, avoids query-conditioned retrieval behavior, and makes matched-backbone comparisons easier to interpret. This design also keeps the contribution focused: the paper claims better use of weak supervision during pretraining, not a more complex inference protocol.

\paragraph{Role of hierarchy in the final formulation.}
Hierarchy plays two complementary training-time roles in the final formulation. First, it acts as an evidence granularity prior: fine-level supervision is expected to concentrate on fewer frames, whereas mid/coarse supervision naturally supports smoother evidence distributions over longer temporal spans. Second, hierarchy acts as a source of explicit cross-level supervision: a nested fine-mid-coarse chain is injected into each mixed-level batch, and the fine caption serves as a local semantic anchor for the denoised mid/coarse representations. This turns hierarchy from a data organization property into a supervision structure. In our view, this is a more faithful use of SurgLaVi-style annotations than simply flattening all levels into one training pool.

\paragraph{Limitations and future directions.}
The method still depends on the quality of the paired text. If a caption is semantically incomplete, temporally drifting, or only loosely related to the local visual content, text-guided reweighting and fine-anchor regularization cannot fully recover the correct evidence. In particular, the present formulation addresses the temporal-alignment side of weak-pair noise more directly than commentary drift or caption corruption. In addition, the current hierarchical regularizer uses one explicit fine-mid-coarse relation per batch rather than exhaustive grounding over all nested captions, which keeps the method lightweight but does not exploit the full hierarchy. Future work could study denser cross-level matching, adaptive policies for selecting informative hierarchical anchors, stronger but still lightweight local matching functions, or curriculum schedules that vary denoising and anchoring strength across training. Another useful direction is to quantify temporal noise more directly with partially refined timestamps so that denoising behavior can be evaluated against localized reference evidence rather than only downstream transfer performance.
