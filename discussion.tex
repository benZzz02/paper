\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What SurgAlign is designed to solve.}
SurgAlign addresses one specific weakness of current surgical VLP pipelines: a weakly paired interval may be semantically valid while still containing only a limited amount of text-supporting visual evidence. The method is therefore best understood as a supervision refinement strategy. It does not replace the need for strong backbones, better corpora, or cleaner captions. Instead, it improves how weak temporal supervision is consumed once such data already exist.

\paragraph{Why training-only denoising is the right scope.}
The temporal noise we study is introduced during weakly supervised pretraining, so it is most naturally handled during pretraining as well. This is why SurgAlign keeps inference unchanged. We do not carry expanded-window reweighting into downstream evaluation, because the claim of the paper is not that surgical retrieval or recognition requires a more complex test-time protocol. The claim is that CLIP-style surgical VLP can be improved by trusting weak temporal positives more carefully during training.

\paragraph{Evidence usage and method contribution.}
An important practical consideration is that temporal expansion alone does not guarantee that the model will actually use the expanded region. For that reason, our evaluation distinguishes between the primary benchmark comparison and an explicit expanded-window evidence analysis that measures how much selection mass is assigned outside the original weak interval. This separation keeps the contribution clear: SurgAlign is not a claim that every frame in the expanded neighborhood is equally useful, but that weak temporal supervision can be consumed more effectively when the model is allowed to recover supportive evidence beyond the noisy annotated boundary.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
SurgAlign assumes that the paired text remains semantically informative even if it is temporally imprecise. If the text itself is highly noisy, incomplete, or only loosely related to the visual content, local reweighting cannot fully recover the correct evidence. The current formulation also models a bounded local temporal offset rather than arbitrary long-range drift. Finally, while the hierarchical consistency term is intentionally lightweight, it is still a heuristic structural prior rather than a full probabilistic model of cross-level alignment.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
There are several natural extensions. One direction is to replace the current similarity-based frame scoring with a stronger but still lightweight cross-modal scorer. Another is to learn adaptive expansion ratios or uncertainty-aware denoising strength instead of fixing them globally. It would also be valuable to evaluate SurgAlign with partially refined timestamp annotations so that denoising quality can be measured directly, rather than only through downstream transfer.
