\begin{abstract}
Surgical video understanding is important for workflow analysis, context-aware assistance, retrospective review, and surgical education, yet dense expert annotation for phases, steps, actions, tools, and triplets remains expensive and difficult to scale. Weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining therefore offers an attractive route to learn transferable representations for zero-shot surgical evaluation. However, most existing CLIP-style pipelines still sample multiple frames from a weakly annotated interval, pool them into one clip embedding, and align that embedding to the paired text as if all sampled frames were equally reliable positives. In surgical videos, this assumption is often too strong: the visual evidence that truly supports a caption is frequently concentrated in only a subset of frames and may be slightly shifted relative to the annotated timestamp. Moreover, hierarchical annotations such as fine, mid, and coarse captions are often flattened into ordinary training pairs, leaving their cross-level relations underused. We address these issues with a train-time window denoising framework with hierarchical anchoring for weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining. During training, we treat each weak interval as a noisy temporal anchor, expand it into a small local neighborhood, and use the paired text to softly reweight frames within the expanded window. The resulting denoised visual view is used as an auxiliary training signal. We further structure each mixed-level batch to contain at least one nested fine-mid-coarse triplet and reuse the fine-level caption as a local semantic anchor for regularizing denoised mid/coarse representations, without adding extra text-encoder passes. Test-time inference remains standard CLIP-style independent video and text encoding. This design preserves the scalability and simplicity of CLIP-based surgical VLP while directly addressing temporal imprecision and converting hierarchical annotations into explicit training-time supervision.
\end{abstract}
