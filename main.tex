\title{SurgAlign: Misalignment-Aware Surgical\\Video-Language Pretraining via Expanded Temporal Evidence and Text-Guided Local Reweighting}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining offers a scalable alternative to dense manual annotation, but its video-text pairs are often only loosely aligned in time. In realistic surgical corpora, paired text commonly comes from narrations, transcripts, or weak temporal descriptions whose visual support may occur slightly before or after the annotated interval center, or may occupy only a short subsegment within that interval. Standard CLIP-style training nevertheless treats the sampled clip as a uniformly reliable positive, which can blur the local evidence needed for fine-grained transfer. We present \textbf{SurgAlign}, a misalignment-aware training framework for surgical video-language pretraining. SurgAlign expands each weak interval into a bounded temporal neighborhood, samples candidate frames from the expanded window, and uses the paired text to compute soft frame weights for a selected local visual view during training. Hierarchical caption levels are incorporated as evidence-granularity priors through level-specific selection sharpness, and same-video adjacent levels can be regularized to remain locally compatible. All additional logic is training-only: test-time inference remains standard CLIP-style independent video and text encoding. SurgAlign is therefore not a new backbone or inference protocol, but a principled way to consume weak temporal supervision more effectively during surgical pretraining.
\end{abstract}
