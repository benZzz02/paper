\begin{abstract}
Surgical video understanding is important for workflow analysis, context-aware assistance, retrospective review, and surgical education, yet dense expert annotation for phases, steps, actions, tools, and triplets remains expensive and difficult to scale. Weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining therefore offers an attractive route to learn transferable representations for zero-shot surgical evaluation. However, most existing CLIP-style pipelines still sample multiple frames from a weakly annotated interval, pool them into one clip embedding, and align that embedding to the paired text as if all sampled frames were equally reliable positives. In surgical videos, this assumption is often too strong: the visual evidence that truly supports a caption is frequently concentrated in only a subset of frames and may be slightly shifted relative to the annotated timestamp. We address this problem with a train-time window denoising framework for weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining. During training, each weak interval is treated as a noisy temporal anchor, expanded into a bounded local neighborhood, and softly reweighted by the paired text to produce a denoised visual training view. Fine/mid/coarse annotation levels are used only as evidence-granularity priors, enabling sharper frame selection for fine captions and smoother selection for broader captions. We validate the framework first on a controlled CLIP baseline built from a surgery-domain-pretrained visual backbone, and then on public surgical VLP initializations retrained on SurgLaVi under a unified protocol. Test-time inference remains standard CLIP-style independent video and text encoding. This design preserves the scalability and simplicity of CLIP-based surgical VLP while directly addressing temporal imprecision during training.
\end{abstract}
