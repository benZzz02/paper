\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented SurgAlign, a training-time method for surgical video-language pretraining that treats a weak interval as a noisy temporal anchor rather than a uniformly positive clip. By combining bounded temporal expansion with text-guided local evidence selection, SurgAlign improves how weak video-text supervision is consumed while keeping standard CLIP-style inference unchanged.

The main takeaway is simple: better modeling of local evidence inside weak temporal pairs can improve zero-shot surgical understanding, especially on tasks that depend on localized visual cues. More broadly, the results suggest that progress in weakly supervised surgical VLP may come not only from larger corpora and stronger backbones, but also from more careful treatment of temporal supervision noise.
