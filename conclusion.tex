\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studies weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining from the perspective of temporal imprecision inside weakly paired intervals and underused cross-level relations inside hierarchical annotations. We argue that a globally valid clip-caption pair does not imply uniformly reliable frame-level supervision, and that hierarchical fine/mid/coarse captions should not be consumed only as additional independent training pairs. To address these issues, we propose a train-time window denoising framework that expands each weak interval into a bounded local neighborhood, uses the paired text to softly reweight candidate frames, and introduces the resulting denoised visual view as an auxiliary training signal. We further convert hierarchy into explicit training-time supervision through multi-positive hierarchical anchoring, where each mid/coarse parent is regularized against all nested fine captions that are visible inside the batch, while preserving standard CLIP-style test-time inference.

The resulting formulation remains intentionally lightweight: it changes how weak supervision is trusted and structured during pretraining without changing the downstream protocol or introducing extra text-encoder passes for hierarchical grounding. This makes the method easy to integrate into existing CLIP-style surgical VLP pipelines and easy to evaluate under matched-backbone conditions. More broadly, the paper highlights that progress in weak surgical VLP should not be framed only as a question of larger datasets or stronger encoders, but also as a question of how temporally noisy positives and hierarchical relations are modeled during training.
