\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studies weakly supervised surgical video-language pretraining from the perspective of temporal imprecision inside weakly paired intervals. We argue that a globally valid clip-caption pair does not imply uniformly reliable frame-level supervision, especially in surgical videos where the most supportive evidence may occupy only a short local span and may drift slightly from the annotated timestamp. To address this issue, we propose a train-time window denoising framework that expands each weak interval into a bounded local neighborhood, uses the paired text to softly reweight candidate frames, and introduces the resulting denoised visual view as an auxiliary training signal. We further use fine/mid/coarse annotation levels only as lightweight evidence-granularity priors inside the same denoising mechanism, while preserving standard CLIP-style test-time inference.

The resulting formulation remains intentionally lightweight: it changes how weak supervision is trusted during pretraining without changing the downstream protocol or introducing extra inference-time complexity. Our experiments validate this story in two complementary settings: a controlled CLIP baseline built from a surgically pretrained visual backbone, and a broader adaptation setting that retrains public surgical VLP initializations on SurgLaVi under the same denoising rule. More broadly, the paper highlights that progress in weak surgical VLP should not be framed only as a question of larger datasets or stronger encoders, but also as a question of how temporally noisy positives are modeled during training.
