\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented SurgAlign, a training-time method for learning from temporally misaligned weak surgical video-text pairs. Instead of treating a weak interval as a uniformly positive clip, SurgAlign treats it as a noisy temporal anchor and combines bounded temporal expansion with text-guided local evidence selection, while keeping standard CLIP-style inference unchanged.

The main takeaway is equally simple: better positive-pair construction can improve zero-shot surgical understanding when weak video-text alignment is locally noisy, especially on tasks that depend on localized visual cues. More broadly, the results suggest that progress in weakly supervised video-language pretraining may depend not only on larger corpora and stronger backbones, but also on more careful treatment of temporal supervision noise.
